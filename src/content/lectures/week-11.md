## The argument

A pay toilet or a turnstile kiosk with "exact change only" combines every
condition this course has been isolating separately: there's no real
alternative, the urgency is real rather than manufactured, and the
embarrassment of being stuck is about as high as a one-shot interface
gets. If any machine this semester makes the argument without needing
much interpretation, it's this one.

"Exact change only" surviving decades of otherwise-relentless redesign
cycles is itself a clue — coin-only mechanisms are cheaper to maintain and
harder to defraud, and a franchise or concession contract is usually
sitting behind that choice, protecting a margin rather than a mechanism.
The harder question this week asks is whether "worse for the user" and
"more profitable for the operator" are actually two separate claims here,
or the same fact stated from two different seats.

**Question to carry:** if the operator redesigned this machine purely for
their own benefit, would anything about it actually change?
