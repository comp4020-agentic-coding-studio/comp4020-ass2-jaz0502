## Outline

- ticket machines as the clearest case of manufactured urgency: a visible
  countdown, an audible cadence, a sightline back to the queue
- the difference between a queue that exists and a queue the interface
  merely implies
- why rushed decisions are worse decisions, and who that reliably benefits

## The argument

A ticket machine's countdown, beep cadence and sightline back to a queue
are not incidental interface chrome — they are deliberately tuned
pressure. A machine that let you take as long as you liked would look
almost identical in every other respect, and would make exactly the same
sale. The urgency is added, not required by the transaction.

That matters because rushed decisions are measurably worse decisions:
people accept the default fare, skip the cheaper option, or buy a ticket
they didn't need, in the seconds before they'd otherwise have caught the
mistake. A queue you only imagine still changes what you choose, whether
or not anyone is actually standing behind you.

> **Question to carry:** if removing the time pressure wouldn't cost the
> machine a single sale, what is it actually for?
