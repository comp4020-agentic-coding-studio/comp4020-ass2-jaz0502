## The brief

> Take apart one machine this course has given you the vocabulary for, then put
> it back together fairer — without pretending it has no legitimate constraints
> at all.

Choose one machine from the semester's twelve, or a close relative of one. The
teardown documents how it currently behaves and who that behaviour serves. The
redesign then has to do something harder than remove friction: it has to say
which piece of friction was actually legitimate, and keep it. The closing memo
names whoever loses out from your redesign — an operator, a vendor, a
franchise holder — and says plainly why they'd push back.

## What you submit

A dossier: the teardown, the redesign (sketches, flows, or a working
prototype — your choice of fidelity), and the resistance memo (300–500 words).
Say plainly what a reader needs to evaluate it without you in the room.
