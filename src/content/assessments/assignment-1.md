## The brief

> Walk one stranger through one machine, in the order it actually happened, then
> diagnose the moment it stopped being obvious.

Pick a single transaction on a single machine — not a category of machine, one
you can name and, ideally, one you can go and use again while you write this.
The walkthrough comes first and stays purely descriptive: what the machine
displayed, what you pressed, where you paused. Diagnosis comes after, and has
to reach for a specific concept from the first three weeks rather than a
general complaint about bad design.

## What you submit

One document, in three clearly labelled parts:

1. **The machine and the transaction** — one sentence naming both, specific
   enough that a reader could go find the same machine themselves.
2. **The walkthrough** (roughly 800–1200 words) — what happened, in the order
   it happened. No diagnosis yet: what the interface displayed, what you
   pressed, and exactly where you paused or guessed.
3. **The diagnosis** — the moment from part 2 where you hesitated, explained
   using one named concept from weeks 1–3 by name, not a general complaint
   about bad design.

Attach at least one photo of the machine's interface at the point you
hesitated, with a caption saying what it shows. If the machine won't let you
photograph it — some ATMs and secure kiosks won't — a photo of the queue,
the signage, or the receipt is an acceptable substitute; say why in the
caption.
